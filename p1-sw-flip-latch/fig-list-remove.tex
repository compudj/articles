% Removing node E from P <-> E <-> N of a bidirectional list, leaving P <-> N.
%
% THREE bands, and the middle one is why. Drawn in two bands, the record table
% sat inside the updater's phase row above the word "build", so it read as a
% per-phase snapshot -- and a reader reasonably asks what it looks like after
% the commit. It has no after-commit state: the records are built once and are
% identical until they are freed. All the change is in the SLOTS, and in one
% selector word.
%
% So the records get a row of their own, bracketed across the whole timeline,
% with the selector beside them -- close, so the "every record reads this one
% word" arrows stay short and local. The phase row below shows only what each
% slot holds, which is the thing that actually moves: direct, parked, parked
% still, direct again. Exactly ONE arrow crosses between bands, the commit
% store, because that is the one event reaching from a phase into the records.
%

\caption{Removing \code{E} from
\code{P}\,$\leftrightarrow$\,\code{E}\,$\leftrightarrow$\,\code{N} of a
bidirectional list, leaving \code{P}\,$\leftrightarrow$\,\code{N}, through the
\fliplatch. The reader's band carries the list: it traverses
\code{P}\,$\leftrightarrow$\,\code{E}\,$\leftrightarrow$\,\code{N} before the
commit and \code{P}\,$\leftrightarrow$\,\code{N} after --- in either direction,
never a half-linked node. The middle band is the \pseudotxn's record set, drawn
once because it is built once and never changes: each moving edge bound to its
\{\textit{old},\,\textit{new}\} values, here \textit{old}\,$=$\,\code{E} and
\textit{new} the bypassing neighbour, all reading the one selector beside them.
The bottom band is where the change is. A slot holds its direct value, then a
tagged pointer into a record once \emph{installed} --- which the commit does not
disturb --- and its new direct value once \emph{settled}. The commit is a single
store into the selector, and it is the only arrow here that reaches from a phase
into the records. Unlike insert, a node leaves the list. At the commit \code{E}
is unlinked, but its own \code{next} and \code{prev} still point at \code{N} and
\code{P}, so a reader already standing on \code{E} reads straight through them;
\code{synchronize\_rcu} waits out the grace period until that reader drains, and
only then is \code{free(E)} --- together with the records --- safe. This
drain-and-free is ordinary \rcu, after the transaction, not part of it
(cf.\ \cref{fig:fliplatch}).}
\label{fig:listremove}
\end{figure}
